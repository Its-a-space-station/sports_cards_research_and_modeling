## Quintile spreads

Recomputed from `class_quintile_spreads.csv` through `spread_summary` (year-block bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, seed 42 — the same independence discipline as the gate). Ungraded hitter 12m frame; spread = mean ret_12m of the top score quintile minus the bottom, per month:

| series | mean_spread | 95% CI | share_positive | n_months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| model_score (walk-forward predictions, CLASS_HOLD_FEATURES) | **+0.5081** | [+0.3886, +0.5864] | **1.000** | 33 (test years 2023-01 → 2025-09) |
| draft_rank | **+0.2828** | [+0.0947, +0.4694] | 0.885 | 52 (2021-06 → 2025-09) |
| surprise_ops | +0.0522 | [−0.1666, +0.3454] | 0.438 | 32 (2021-05 → 2025-09) |

- **draft_rank sign nuance (mandatory):** the spread is computed on the raw score, so the top quintile of draft_rank is the **largest rank numbers — the latest draft positions / undrafted players**. The +0.2828 spread therefore means **late-pick cards outperformed early-pick cards** at 12m in this sample (descriptive), not that high draft pedigree wins.
- **surprise_ops is a low-power null, not evidence of absence:** surprise_ops covers only 10.2% of the registered frame structurally (7.8% panel-wide), so its spread is measured on a ~10%-covered subsample (median 78 scored cards/month vs 754 for model_score); the null CI is what low power looks like.
- model_score's CI rests on the same 3 year blocks as the gate — wide by construction; its share_positive 1.0 over 33 months is consistent with the gate's positive yearly means.

## Comparison to P6c

Same gate shape, opposite verdict:

| | P6c (`docs/findings/2026-09-17-multiyear-modeling.md`) | T2-model (this doc) |
|---|---|---|
| universe | 39 players (33 reach `panel_multiyear.parquet`; 59 cards; 6 fully absent from price data) | 1,639 cards = 1,639 players (`panel_class.parquet`); registered frame 26,640 rows / 1,009 unique ungraded hitter cards |
| registered cell | 12m, ungraded, top-5/year, year-block bootstrap, 0.14 log fees | identical shape (same reviewed harness, `walkforward_holds.py`) |
| verdict | **FAIL** — mean_excess +0.2417, net_ci_low −0.2816, n_years 3 | **PASS** — mean_excess +2.6714, net_ci_low +0.6445, n_years 3 |
| pick concentration | 5 distinct players across 15 picks (~3/year) | 7 distinct cards across 15 picks (5/1/2 per year; one card = 9/15 rows) |

What changed: **(a) breadth** — ~40× more cards and players, and the prospect stage (structurally unobservable in P6c) is 71.9% of the T2 registered frame; **(b) features** — T2 adds the expectations/surprise block (surprise_ops / surprise_era_flipped, draft_rank) and class-year semantics on top of P6c's persistence/career features; **(c) the concentration caveat that keeps the comparison honest** — both verdicts rest on 3 year blocks with a handful of distinct picks, so the flip from FAIL to PASS is not evidence that breadth or surprise features manufacture a diversified edge; the T2 PASS is exactly as concentration-fragile as the P6c FAIL was, in the opposite direction.

## Caveats

- **3 year blocks:** the registered verdict and the model-score quintile CI each rest on 3 yearly blocks (2023–2025); the block bootstrap over years is weak by construction, and a PASS or FAIL at this power is fragile either way.
- **Full-frame median imputation on the descriptive importance fits** (`run_class_importance.py`, documented in its docstring): medians pool information across time, biasing the importance fits **toward inflating apparent fit quality** (rankings optimistic, not out-of-sample estimates). The pre-registered gate is unaffected — it imputes and scales from train years only, per fold.
- **prospect_rank uncollected:** 0.0000 coverage verified in `panel_class.parquet` (Pipeline blocked; cron backfill id `01M2WHDF834CTJB046PW9J3TFH` pending). Recorded as uncollected, never "unranked"; excluded from all feature sets.
- **age_at_debut NaN on prospect rows by design:** the Task-1 look-ahead fix (commit `3b63e81`) masks age_at_debut to NaN when the debut is after the entry month; 71.9% of the registered frame is prospect-stage and 99.9% of those rows carry NaN age_at_debut, flowing through the gate's per-fold train-median imputation like any other NaN.
- **surprise_era sign flip for pitchers:** surprise_era is positive = worse-than-expected (opposite sign to surprise_ops), so the pitcher feature set uses `surprise_era_flipped = −surprise_era` (positive = better); the descriptive pitcher cell is also small-n (12,154 rows, 415 cards vs 26,640 / 1,009 for hitters).
- **class-year rookie_year semantics:** in panel_class, `rookie_year` carries the card's **class year**, not the player's MLB rookie season; `years_since_class = season_year(entry_month) − rookie_year` is measured from the class.
- **Row-level top-k repeated-card behavior is reviewed harness behavior shared with P6c:** monthly panel rows, no per-card dedup within a year; P6c's findings doc carries the same caveat (5 distinct players across 15 picks).
- **Surprise coverage is structural:** surprise_ops 7.8% / surprise_era 3.9% panel-wide (10.2% / 34.4%-draft_rank in the registered frame) — surprise conclusions are low-power by construction, in both the importance fits and the quintile spreads.
- **Descriptive-cell artifacts:** the 36m cell's n_years=1 degenerate CI and the 6m/2024 yearly of exactly 0.0 (same-card artifact) are flagged in their section; neither bears on the verdict.
- **No-look-ahead controls verified:** adapter performs no imputation (NaNs pass through raw); gate imputation/scaling is train-only per fold; quintile model scores come only from each test year's own walk-forward model; the quintile runner's positional join is hard-verified through the realized identity (`np.allclose` assert passed on the real run); age_at_debut masked when debut is after entry.
